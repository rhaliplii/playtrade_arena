Trading glossary

Every term from the Academy in plain English. Tap one for an example, related terms and the lesson that teaches it.

106 terms

#

  • 1% ruleRisk at most about 1% (up to 2%) of your account on any one trade: the loss if your stop is hit, not the size of the position. Risk

A

  • Analyst estimateThe expected numbers analysts publish before results. Above it is a beat, below it a miss; the price moves on the gap. Fundamentals
  • AskThe lowest price a seller will accept right now. When you buy straight away, you buy at the ask. Markets
  • Asset allocationHow you split money between stocks, bonds and cash. For most people it matters more than any single pick. Portfolio
  • Asset classA family of things you can trade that behave alike: bonds, stocks, commodities, forex and crypto. Broad index ETFs count as a sixth group here: a basket that can hold any of them. Markets

B

  • BearishExpecting prices to fall. Like a bear swiping its paws down. Markets
  • Bear marketA sustained fall, often defined as about 20% or more down from a recent high. A bear expects prices to fall. Markets
  • BenchmarkAn index that matches what you hold. It’s the bar your results must clear, after all costs. Portfolio
  • BidThe highest price a buyer will pay right now. When you sell straight away, you sell at the bid. Markets
  • BondA loan to a government or company. It pays interest and sits at the calm end of the risk ladder. Markets
  • BreakoutWhen price moves through a level it couldn’t pass before, such as above resistance. More believable on high volume. Charts
  • BullishExpecting prices to rise. Like a bull tossing its horns up. Markets
  • Bull marketA sustained rise, often defined as about 20% or more up from a recent low. A bull expects prices to rise. Markets

C

  • CandlestickOne bar on a price chart that shows four prices for its period: open, high, low and close. Green closed above its open, red below. Charts
  • Cash flowThe money that really came in, minus what really went out. Bills are paid in cash, not in profit. Fundamentals
  • Central bankThe bank that sets a country’s key interest rate, like the Federal Reserve in the US. It raises rates to cool inflation and cuts them to support growth. Economy
  • ChannelA trendline plus a parallel line through the highs. Price often travels between the two, like a lane. Charts
  • CommissionA broker’s fee per trade or per share. Often zero now, but “free” brokers may earn it back through wider spreads. Costs
  • ConfluenceSeveral different kinds of evidence agreeing, like trend, level and volume. Indicators built from the same prices don’t count twice. Charts
  • CorrectionA fall of about 10% from a recent high. Smaller than a bear market, which is about 20% or more. Markets
  • CorrelationHow two assets move together, from −1 (opposite) to +1 (in lockstep). Assets that rise and fall as one don’t protect each other. Portfolio
  • CPIThe Consumer Price Index: the cost of a basket of everyday goods, used to measure inflation. A monthly, high-impact release. Economy

D

  • Day tradingTrades held for minutes to hours and all closed by the bell. It needs hours at the screen while the market is open. Markets
  • DiversificationSpreading money across many holdings, so one bad pick hurts a little, not a lot. Portfolio
  • DividendPart of a company’s profit paid out to its owners, the shareholders. Not every company pays one. Fundamentals
  • DojiA candle that opens and closes at almost the same price. Neither buyers nor sellers won: indecision. Charts
  • Double bottomTwo failed tries at the same low. Confirmed only on a close above the middle high between them. Charts
  • Double topTwo failed tries at the same high. Confirmed only on a close below the middle low between them. Charts
  • DowntrendA market making lower highs and lower lows. Charts
  • DrawdownThe fall from a peak in your account or portfolio to the lowest point after it. Portfolio

E

  • EarningsA company’s profit, reported every quarter. Earnings day often brings big price moves and overnight gaps. Fundamentals
  • Economic calendarA list of when key economic data comes out, such as the jobs report, CPI and rate decisions, with each release’s expected impact. Economy
  • Economic cycleThe way economies grow, slow, shrink and recover. Some sectors have tended to do better in certain phases, but every cycle is different. Economy
  • Engulfing candleA candle whose body swallows the whole body of the one before it. Bullish engulfing: after a fall, a big green body swallows a red one. Charts
  • Entry ruleOne sentence that says exactly what must happen before you buy, so you can follow it the same way every time. Charts
  • EPS (earnings per share)A company’s profit ÷ its number of shares. Fundamentals
  • ETFExchange-traded fund: a basket of many assets that trades like a single stock. It’s as risky as what it holds; a broad index ETF is a calm place to start. Markets
  • ExpectancyWhat a strategy makes per trade, on average, over many trades: (win rate × average win) − (loss rate × average loss). Risk

F

  • Fear and greedThe two feelings behind most trading mistakes. Fear closes winners early and skips good setups; greed trades too big and holds past the target. Psychology
  • FOMOFear of missing out: the itch to jump in because a price is flying and everyone else is in. It makes you buy after the move, not before it. Psychology

G

  • GapWhen a price opens far from the previous close, with no trades in between. A gap can jump straight past your stop. Risk
  • GuidanceWhat a company says it expects next, such as next year’s sales. It often moves the price more than the results. Fundamentals

H

  • HammerA candle with a small body near the top and a long lower wick, after a fall. Buyers fought back from the lows. Charts
  • Head and shouldersThree peaks: a left shoulder, a higher head and a lower right shoulder. It counts only on a close through the neckline. Charts
  • High-frequency trading (HFT)Robots that trade in millionths of a second. Their edge is speed, so don’t race them on tiny moves. Markets

I

  • IndexA number that tracks a group of stocks, like the S&P 500. It shows how the whole group is doing. Markets
  • InflationHow fast prices rise. Most countries track it with the CPI. Economy
  • Interest rateThe price of borrowing money. The central bank sets the key rate; when it rises, loans cost more and cash pays more. Economy

L

  • LeverageControlling a bigger position than your money, using borrowed funds. It multiplies gains, losses and stress. Risk
  • Limit orderAn order to trade only at your price or better. You get your price, but it may never fill. Orders
  • LiquidityHow easily you can buy or sell without moving the price. Liquid markets have many orders and tight spreads. Markets
  • LongBuying to profit from a rise: buy first, sell later. Result = (sell price − buy price) × shares. Orders
  • Loss aversionA loss hurts about twice as much as an equal gain feels good. It tempts you to hold losers and sell winners early. Psychology
  • Loss limitA circuit breaker: once you lose this much in a day or a week, you stop trading until the next day or week. Risk

M

  • MACDThe gap between a fast (12-day) and a slow (26-day) exponential average. A 9-day signal line and bars show momentum building or fading. Charts
  • MarginThe deposit needed to open a leveraged position. Position = margin × leverage. Risk
  • Margin callA demand from your broker to add money when losses eat into your margin. It comes before your margin is gone, and the broker may close your trade. Risk
  • Market makerA firm that is always ready to buy and sell, quoting both a bid and an ask. It earns the spread between them. Markets
  • Market orderAn order to trade right now at the best available price. You get speed, not a set price. Orders
  • Market valueWhat the market says a whole company is worth: share price × all the shares. Also called market cap. Fundamentals
  • Moving averageThe average close of the last N days, redrawn each day. It smooths price into one line and lags: it turns after price does. Charts

N

  • NecklineThe line joining the lows between the peaks of a head and shoulders. A close through it confirms the pattern. Charts

O

  • Open, high, low, closeThe four prices in every candle: where the period started, its highest and lowest price, and where it ended. Charts
  • OverboughtRSI above 70: price rose fast. Fast isn’t finished, so it isn’t a sell signal on its own. Charts
  • OversoldRSI below 30: price fell fast. It can keep falling, so it isn’t a buy signal on its own. Charts

P

  • P/E ratioShare price ÷ EPS: how many dollars buyers pay for $1 of yearly profit. A high P/E means high expectations. Fundamentals
  • Position sizeHow much you buy. Size it so a stop-out or a normal gap costs a small, fixed share of your account. Risk
  • Position tradingTrades held for weeks to months. You check them about once a week. Markets
  • Pre-trade checklist5 to 7 yes/no checks you run before every trade. Each check is a fact. One “no” means no trade. Risk
  • Priced inAlready reflected in the price. If everyone expected the news, the price has likely moved already, so the news itself barely moves it. Economy
  • ProfitWhat’s left of revenue after every cost is paid. Fundamentals
  • Profit marginProfit ÷ revenue: how much of each dollar of sales the company keeps. Fundamentals

R

  • Real returnYour return minus inflation: what your money can actually buy. Economy
  • RebalancingBringing your split back to its target: trim what grew, top up what lagged. Once or twice a year is enough for most people. Portfolio
  • ResistanceA price zone where sellers stepped in before: a ceiling. Once it breaks, it often turns into support. Charts
  • Revenge tradingRushing into a bigger trade to win back a loss. It usually turns one bad trade into a bad day. Psychology
  • RevenueAll the money a company takes in from sales, before any costs. Fundamentals
  • Reward-to-riskWhat a trade could make compared with what it could lose: (target − entry) ÷ (entry − stop). Pros aim for 2:1 or more. Risk
  • Round tripOne buy plus one sell of the same position. Costs like the spread and commission are counted per round trip. Costs
  • RSI (Relative Strength Index)A 0–100 speed gauge comparing recent up days with recent down days, usually over 14 days. It measures speed, not value. Charts

S

  • SectorA group of companies in the same line of business, such as tech, energy, utilities or consumer staples. Economy
  • ShareA slice of ownership in a company. Owners share in its profits and usually get a vote. Fundamentals
  • ShortSelling borrowed shares to profit from a fall, then buying them back. A short’s loss has no ceiling. Orders
  • Short squeezeA fast rise in a heavily shorted stock as short sellers rush to buy back. Prices have doubled in days. Risk
  • Sideways market (range)A market with no new highs or lows: price bounces between a floor and a ceiling. Charts
  • SlippageThe gap between the price you expected and the price you got. Biggest in fast markets and with market orders. Now and then it goes your way. Costs
  • SpreadThe gap between the bid and the ask. You pay it on every round trip, even if the price never moves. Costs
  • Stop-limit“When the price hits X, place a limit order at Y.” It controls your price, but in a crash it may not fill at all. Orders
  • Stop-lossAn order that closes a trade at a pre-set loss level. Set it the moment you open the trade. Orders
  • Stop orderAn order that waits for a trigger price, then becomes a market order. A buy stop sits above the price, a sell stop below. Orders
  • SupportA price zone where buyers stepped in before: a floor. It’s a zone, not an exact line, and broken support often turns into resistance. Charts
  • SwapOvernight financing on a leveraged forex or CFD position, usually a cost, sometimes a small credit. On US stock margin it’s called margin interest. Costs
  • SwingA peak or a dip on a chart. Compare each swing with the last to read the trend. Charts
  • Swing tradingTrades held for days to weeks. You check them about once a day, often after the close. Markets

T

  • TiltThe angry state after a loss that drives revenge trading: bigger size, faster trades, setups you’d normally skip. Poker players named it. Psychology
  • TimeframeHow much time one candle covers: 1 minute, 1 hour, 1 day or 1 week. Same stock, same prices, different story. Charts
  • Trading journalA record of every trade: the setup, entry, stop, target, result and how you felt. You read it in your weekly review. Psychology
  • Trading planYour written rules for trading: your style and markets, when you enter, where you exit, how much you risk, your checklist and your weekly review. Risk
  • Trading styleHow long you hold a trade: day trading (minutes to hours), swing trading (days to weeks) or position trading (weeks to months). Markets
  • TrendlineA straight line under at least two rising lows (or over two falling highs), extended to the right. A break is a warning, not a signal. Charts

U

  • UptrendA market making higher highs and higher lows. It lasts until that pattern breaks. Charts

V

  • VolatilityHow much and how fast a price swings. Higher volatility means bigger moves, both up and down. Risk
  • VolumeHow many shares changed hands in a period. Moves on high volume have more people behind them; volume shows strength, not direction. Charts

W

  • Weekly reviewOnce a week you read your journal: did you follow every rule, your wins and losses, your biggest mistake, and one change or none. Psychology
  • WickThe thin line above or below a candle’s body. It reaches the high or the low: how far price went before it was pushed back. Charts
  • Win rateThe share of your trades that make money. On its own it says little: a high win rate can hide a losing strategy. Risk
